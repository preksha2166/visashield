## FBI warning: imposters targeting foreign students (May 2025)
In alert I-051325-PSA, dated May 13, 2025, the FBI warned about criminals targeting foreign students who are in the United States on valid F-1 visas. The scammers falsely claim the student has broken visa rules or has another immigration problem. They then threaten prosecution or deportation to get money. The alert specifically named students from the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Jordan as targets.

## Who the student-visa scammers pretend to be
According to the FBI, these scammers pose as agents of DHS, Homeland Security Investigations or USCIS. In some versions they claim to be officials of foreign governments such as the UAE or Qatar, or diplomats from the UAE Embassy in Washington, DC. They may also pose as US universities. The FBI says they spoof the phone numbers of government agencies, foreign embassies or universities. They may speak professionally and use the accent or language of the place they claim to represent.

## What the student-visa scammers ask for
Victims are told to send money to an unknown person or bank account. The scammers say the money is for processing immigration paperwork, paying university registration fees or covering legal fees. The FBI advises hanging up and calling the agency back on an official number. Be wary of look-alike web addresses such as "gov.org" that copy real .gov domains. Never share two-factor authentication codes, and do not download files from unverified sources.

## If you sent money to a student-visa scammer
The FBI asks victims to file a complaint at www.ic3.gov with as much detail as possible. If you made a bank transfer, contact your bank right away. Ask the bank to try to recall the transfer and to notify the FBI's Financial Fraud Kill Chain. When filing, include details such as the phone numbers, email addresses, account numbers, messages and payment receipts connected to the scam. The FBI's advice is to contact the bank immediately rather than wait.

## AI voice messages impersonating senior US officials
In alert I-051525-PSA, dated May 15, 2025, the FBI said that since April 2025 criminals had been impersonating senior US officials. They used text messages ("smishing") and AI-generated voice messages ("vishing") to build trust. Then they asked targets to click links or move to another messaging platform. The FBI noted that AI voice cloning can sound almost identical to a real person. Callers should be verified independently, not trusted by voice alone.
Source: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250515

## Protecting yourself from voice and text impersonation
The FBI recommends looking up the official contact details of anyone who claims to be an official and calling them back yourself. Check email addresses, phone numbers, URLs and spelling for small changes. Look for flaws in images and videos, such as distorted hands or unnatural faces. Do not send money or cryptocurrency to someone you have only met online or by phone. Turn on multi-factor authentication, never share one-time codes, and consider a secret phrase with family.
Source: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250515

## Fake FBI agents and fake IC3 sites (July 2026)
On July 20, 2026, the FBI issued alert I-072026-PSA about criminals posing as FBI staff and as IC3 itself. They target people who were already scammed. In one version, a fake "FBI agent" contacts victims on Facebook Messenger or Telegram and sends a link to "update" an IC3 complaint. The link steals information or money. In another version, deepfake videos of senior FBI leaders send viewers to a fake copy of the ic3.gov site that collects contact and loss details.
Source: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260720

## What the FBI and IC3 will not do
The July 2026 alert says the FBI and IC3 do not reach out to victims through social media, Telegram or other messaging apps. They do not charge fees or ask for payment to recover lost money. They do not investigate crimes or return funds through social platforms. To file a real complaint, type www.ic3.gov straight into the browser instead of clicking search results or sponsored ads, and check that the address ends in .gov.
Source: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260720

## Signs of a deepfake video or call
The FBI lists several signs that a video or call may be AI-generated. Look for distorted hands, feet or faces, wrong shadows, odd watermarks and unnatural movement. On calls, listen for lag or a strange speaking rhythm. Voice cloning can sound almost exactly like someone you know. Small changes in names, URLs or email addresses are another clue. Deepfake scams often try to make you feel strong fear or urgency so you act before you check.
Source: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260720
