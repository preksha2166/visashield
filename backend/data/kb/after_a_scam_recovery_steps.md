## If you paid with a credit or debit card
Contact the company or bank that issued the card right away. Use the phone number on the back of the card or the issuer's official app or website. Tell them the charge was fraudulent and ask them to reverse it and refund you. The FTC notes that federal law gives protections against unauthorized credit card use, and debit cards have similar protections. Acting quickly matters.

## If you paid by bank transfer, Zelle or a payment app
If you sent money from your bank account, including with Zelle, report it to your bank or credit union right away. Ask them to reverse the payment and refund you. If you used a payment app such as Venmo, Cash App or PayPal, report the fraudulent transaction to the app company immediately and ask for a reversal. In every case, the FTC's advice is to contact the company that moved the money as soon as you realize it was a scam.

## If you paid by wire transfer or gift card
For a wire through a service such as Western Union or MoneyGram, contact the service immediately. Tell them it was a fraudulent transfer and ask for a refund. MoneyGram's number is 1-800-666-3947 and Western Union's is 1-800-325-6000. For gift cards, contact the company that issued the card using the number on the back. Explain that a scammer got the card number and PIN, ask for a refund, and keep the card and receipt.

## If you paid with cryptocurrency or cash
Contact the crypto exchange or crypto ATM company you used. Report the fraudulent transaction and ask them to reverse it, then report it to law enforcement. The FTC warns that cryptocurrency payments do not have the legal protections that credit and debit cards have. If you mailed cash, contact the US Postal Inspection Service at 1-877-876-2455 right away and ask about stopping the package. If you used another delivery service, contact that company.

## If you gave a scammer your SSN or passwords
If a scammer has your Social Security number, go to IdentityTheft.gov. You can report the theft and get a personal recovery plan. If the number has not been misused yet, the site's data breach steps explain how to protect it. If you gave out a username and password, change the password right away, use a strong new one, and turn on two-factor authentication. If you gave remote access to your device, update your security software, run a scan and remove any threats.

## Tell the FTC about the scam
The FTC asks people to report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. It uses these reports to bring cases against scammers, spot trends, warn consumers and track what is happening in different communities. Reporting a scam to the FTC is separate from asking your bank or payment service for a refund, so do both. Immigration-related scams can also be reported to the ICE Tip Line (1-866-347-2423) and online crime to the FBI at ic3.gov.

## Warning signs of identity theft
The FTC lists common signs that someone is using your identity. You may stop getting a bill, which can mean someone changed your billing address. You may see charges or new bills you do not recognize, or money taken from your bank account that you did not approve. Your credit report may show accounts you never opened. Anyone who shared passport, SSN or bank details with a scam caller should watch for these signs.
Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft

## Reporting identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov
Report identity theft to the FTC at IdentityTheft.gov, or RobodeIdentidad.gov in Spanish. You can also call 877-438-4338 and press 3 for other languages. You will get a personal recovery plan with steps to follow. The site covers more than 30 types of identity theft, including tax identity theft, where a thief uses your SSN to file a tax return and take your refund. Free credit reports are available at annualcreditreport.com.
Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft

## Credit freeze
A credit freeze stops anyone, including you, from opening new credit in your name until you lift it. It is free to place and free to lift. You must contact each of the three national credit bureaus, Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, separately. The freeze stays in place until you remove it. When you need to apply for credit, you can lift it temporarily, either at all three bureaus or only at the one a lender will check.
Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts

## Fraud alerts
A fraud alert tells businesses to check your identity before opening credit in your name. It is free. To place an initial fraud alert, you contact just one of the three credit bureaus, and it must tell the other two. An initial alert lasts one year and can be renewed. Identity theft victims who have an FTC identity theft report or a police report can get an extended fraud alert, which lasts seven years.
Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts

## IRS Identity Protection PIN
An IRS Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN) is a six-digit number. It stops anyone else from filing a federal tax return with your SSN or ITIN. Anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify their identity can get one. The fastest way is through your IRS online account. Other options are Form 15227, which has income limits, or an in-person visit to a Taxpayer Assistance Center. The IP PIN is valid for one calendar year, and a new one is issued each year.
Source: https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin

## The IRS never asks for your IP PIN
The IRS says it will never ask for your IP PIN. Any phone call, email or text asking for it is a scam. Share the IP PIN only with your trusted tax preparer, and use it on every federal tax return you file that year. Anyone with an SSN or ITIN whose personal details were exposed in a scam can get an IP PIN to help guard against tax identity theft.
Source: https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin
