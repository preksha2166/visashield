## OPT basics for F-1 students
Optional Practical Training (OPT) lets F-1 students work in jobs related to their studies. Students can get up to 12 months of OPT at each education level. Any pre-completion OPT used reduces the post-completion time left. To apply, the student's DSO must first enter an OPT recommendation in SEVIS. The student then files Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization, with USCIS. All OPT work must be directly related to the student's major field of study.
Source: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students

## OPT filing window and start of work
A student may file for post-completion OPT up to 90 days before finishing the program. The filing must be no later than 60 days after completing the degree, and only after the DSO has entered the recommendation in SEVIS. A student may not start pre- or post-completion OPT work until USCIS approves Form I-765 and the student has the Employment Authorization Document (EAD). Post-completion OPT may be part time, at least 20 hours a week, or full time.
Source: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students

## STEM OPT extension length and filing
Students with a qualifying STEM degree can apply for a 24-month extension of post-completion OPT. USCIS says to file up to 90 days before the current OPT authorization ends, and within 60 days after the DSO enters the STEM OPT recommendation in SEVIS. If the extension is filed on time and the current OPT ends while it is pending, work authorization is automatically extended for 180 days.

## Unemployment day limits
During the first period of post-completion OPT, a student may have up to 90 days of unemployment. The STEM OPT extension adds 60 more days, so the limit is 150 days across post-completion OPT and STEM OPT combined. SEVP's STEM OPT guidance gives the same numbers: 90 days during the first post-completion OPT period plus 60 extra days during the 24-month extension. Students track their employment and report it through their DSO or the SEVP Portal.

## STEM OPT employer requirements
A STEM OPT employer must be enrolled in E-Verify, the USCIS online system for confirming employment eligibility. The employer must also have a valid Employer Identification Number (EIN). It must complete Form I-983, Training Plan for STEM OPT Students, with the student, and have a real (bona fide) employer-employee relationship with the student. It must pay the student at a level comparable to similarly situated US workers.

## No "employer in name only" or volunteer STEM OPT
USCIS says a STEM OPT employer may not be the student's employer in name only. The student also may not work for the employer as a "volunteer". The SEVP STEM OPT overview adds that STEM OPT students must work at least 20 hours a week for each employer. The employer must provide formal training and learning objectives. An offer of unpaid STEM OPT, or of a letter that lists a company as employer without real work, does not meet these rules.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/additional-resources/stem-opt-extension-overview

## Students cannot sign their own Form I-983
SEVP has reminded students that the STEM OPT employer must give all the employer signatures on Form I-983. A STEM OPT student cannot sign the employer attestation on their own behalf. Start-up businesses can take part, but only if they meet all the regulatory requirements and are enrolled in E-Verify. Be suspicious of any "employer" who asks the student to fill in or sign the employer section.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2016/09/reminder-stem-opt-students-must-train-bona-fide-employers

## STEM OPT reporting duties
STEM OPT students must check in with their DSO every six months to confirm that their SEVIS information is correct. This includes legal name, address, employer name and address, and employment status. Email reminders are sent 30 days before each check-in is due. Changes, including losing a job, must be reported to the DSO within 10 days. Students must also complete self-evaluations at 12 and 24 months, signed by both student and employer.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/for-students/students-stem-opt-reporting-requirements

## Changing employers on STEM OPT
When a STEM OPT student starts a new job, they must submit a new Form I-983 to the DSO within 10 days of starting. The new employer must also be enrolled in E-Verify. A final evaluation for the previous employer is due within 10 days after that job ends. Students must also report big changes to the training plan, such as a new employer EIN, a pay cut not linked to hours, or a large cut in hours.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/for-students/students-stem-opt-reporting-requirements

## The SEVP Portal for OPT reporting
The SEVP Portal lets students on post-completion OPT and STEM OPT meet their reporting duties. Through it they can update their address, phone number and employer details. After OPT is approved in SEVIS, the portal emails the student a link to create an account. Students can still edit their contact and employment details for up to 15 days after their OPT end date. DSOs help with the process but do not create portal accounts themselves.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/sevis-and-the-sevp-portal

## Checking whether an employer uses E-Verify
Anyone can look up employers enrolled in E-Verify with the E-Verify Employer Search tool on e-verify.gov. Results show the employer's legal name, any "doing business as" name, whether the account is enrolled or terminated, enrollment dates, workforce size and hiring site states. The data is reported by employers themselves. A search by trade name may fail if the company enrolled under its legal name, and not every location of a company may take part.
Source: https://www.e-verify.gov/e-verify-employer-search
