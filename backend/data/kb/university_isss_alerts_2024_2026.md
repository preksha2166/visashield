## CU Boulder, June 2026: fake USCIS "AR-11" calls and emails
In June 2026, CU Boulder's ISSS described an ongoing scam about Form AR-11, the change-of-address form. Callers whose caller ID shows USCIS say the student never filed an AR-11 and threaten immediate deportation. They ask for photos of immigration documents, contact details, bank information and Social Security numbers, and use pressure to keep victims on the line. Follow-up emails use a gov-looking display name but come from a Gmail address. ISSS stresses that real officials will not stop you from contacting your school.
Source: https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2026/06/01/scam-alert-uscis-ar-11-phone-call-and-email

## CU Boulder, June 2026: spoofed ICE and police calls
A second June 2026 CU Boulder alert describes a two-step scam. First, a caller spoofing a New York ICE office number claims there is an arrest warrant because of an error in the student's I-94 record and threatens removal. Then a call spoofing the Boulder Police non-emergency line warns of a warrant and a visit to the student's home. Callers may give fake names and "ICE badge numbers" and ask for documents and bank details. ISSS advises hanging up and reporting the call.
Source: https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2026/06/01/scam-alert-spoofed-calls-ice-and-boulder-police-department

## CU Boulder, October 2024: "unpaid university fee" calls
CU Boulder warned in October 2024 about calls spoofed to look like they came from the university. The callers told international students they owed an unpaid CU Boulder fee and would be deported immediately if they did not pay. They first asked for passport and visa details, then told students to send money through Zelle. ISSS said any threatening call like this claiming to be from CU Boulder is fraudulent. It asked students to report such calls to ISSS and to the HSI tip line.
Source: https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2024/10/18/scam-alert-unpaid-cu-boulder-fees-phone-scam

## Genuine SEVP unemployment warnings can also sound urgent
Not every urgent message is a scam. In May 2025, CU Boulder ISSS explained that SEVP may email OPT students who are near or over the unemployment limit. These real messages can say that SEVP will mark the SEVIS record "terminated" if it is not updated within 15 days. They do not ask for payment. ISSS advised students to check the email linked to their SEVP Portal account, report employment promptly, and contact ISSS if they are unsure whether a message is real.
Source: https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2025/05/27/optional-practical-training-opt-and-unemployment

## Wayne State, April 2025: fake CBP and ICE calls with gift cards
In April 2025, Wayne State University's Office of International Programs described a student who got a call from someone claiming to be from Customs and Border Protection. The caller said the student had not reported a change of address and threatened prison and deportation. A second caller posed as ICE and also threatened to freeze the student's bank accounts. The student was told to buy gift cards, and the scammers kept asking for more. The office lists secrecy demands and "your SEVIS is terminated" claims as red flags.
Source: https://oip.wayne.edu/news/scam-emails-and-calls-target-international-students-and-scholars-66119

## SJSU: a catalog of scam scripts
San Jose State's ISSS keeps a list of scams reported by students. They include callers posing as the Social Security Administration, saying a student's SSN was used in crimes, and USCIS imposters who ask about the AR-11 along with SSN digits, passport and bank details. Others are DHS callers with spoofed caller ID who demand a fine in Apple or eBay gift cards, "911" callers who want Google Play cards, and calls claiming to be from the Chinese consulate. A fake USCIS email came from a non-government ".link"-style address.
Source: https://sjsu.edu/isss/resources/scam-alerts.php

## SJSU: academic-integrity and job scams
SJSU also flagged scams that are not government imposters. One online message claimed the student had an academic integrity violation, demanded payment and threatened expulsion. SJSU noted that real conduct notices come only through its official student conduct system. Another scam used Google Chat to pose as HR staff at real companies. They offered jobs with no interview or background check and then sent a check to buy "home office equipment", a fake check pattern.
Source: https://sjsu.edu/isss/resources/scam-alerts.php

## Drexel, February 2026: relaying the DHS advisory
In February 2026, Drexel's international student office passed on the DHS advisory about scammers posing as DHS officials. It listed the usual tactics: asking for A-numbers or I-94s, claiming SEVIS problems, demanding wire, gift card, prepaid card, credit card or crypto payments, and offering to fix status for a fee. Drexel told students not to share information or send money, to end the contact, tell their DSO, report to the ICE Tip Line, and contact campus public safety.
Source: https://drexel.edu/studentlife/student-success/international-student-support/announcements/2026/February/DHS-Advisory-Scams

## University of Washington, December 2024: fake academic integrity office
In December 2024, UW's International Student Services warned about emails from a made-up "UW Academic Integrity Department". The emails accused students of academic violations and suggested their visa status was at risk. The scammers copied the ISS newsletter header to look official. UW advised students not to respond or share personal information, to check the sender through official UW channels, and to report suspicious messages to ISS or the UW Police Department.
Source: https://iss.washington.edu/alert-new-scam-targets-international-students/

## Common threads across university alerts
Across these 2024-2026 alerts, the same patterns repeat. Spoofed caller ID shows real agency, police or university numbers. Scammers make claims about AR-11, I-94, SEVIS or unpaid fees, and threaten arrest, deportation or frozen accounts. They demand gift cards, Zelle, wire transfers or crypto, ask for passport, SSN and bank details, and tell victims to stay on the line or keep quiet. Every office gives the same advice: hang up, do not pay, and contact your international student office directly.
