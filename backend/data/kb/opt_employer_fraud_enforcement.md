## SEVP broadcast on STEM OPT employer fraud (March 2026)
On March 23, 2026, SEVP sent Broadcast Message 2603-02, "STEM OPT Employer Fraud", to all SEVIS users. It asks DSOs to watch for fraud by STEM OPT employers, especially IT recruitment firms, consulting firms and staffing agencies. SEVP said it is working with DHS partners to investigate fraud among these businesses. It repeated a point from the 2016 STEM OPT rule: these kinds of employment arrangements are a fraud concern and are generally not a good fit for STEM OPT.

## What investigators found at suspect employers
During recent site visits, SEVP and HSI found many signs of fraud at suspect STEM OPT employers. Business locations were empty and unused. Some employers listed private homes as worksites, but no employees were there. Human resources and payroll staff were "offshore". Company officials knew little about the business, or made inconsistent and contradictory statements about it and about the students. Many students said they were hired on STEM OPT but never actually showed up for work. Phone numbers did not work.

## Red flags of a fraudulent OPT employer
SEVP lists red flags for possibly fraudulent employers. The company website is vague, with no examples of client work, contact details or social media. The job duties do not match what the employer told the school. The work location does not look suitable or does not match the address on Form I-983. A little-known employer hires a large group of F or M students from one school. The work is done remotely or through apps such as WeChat.

## Consequences and how to report employer fraud
SEVP warns that employers and students involved in suspected fraud risk being referred for administrative or criminal investigation by HSI and the Department of Justice. DSOs are asked to remind students to contact them with questions about a recruiter's advice. Students or DSOs who think an employer is not following the rules can report it to the ICE Tip Line, the SEVP Response Center (703-603-3400 or 1-800-892-4829, SEVP@ice.dhs.gov) or a local Project Campus Sentinel agent.

## Reporting wage and working-condition problems
SEVP's March 2026 message also tells students they can report concerns about wages or working conditions directly to the US Department of Labor. That is separate from reporting suspected immigration fraud to ICE or SEVP. So a student who is not paid as promised, or whose working conditions seem wrong, has an official federal agency to contact. The SRC is open Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern, except federal holidays.

## ICE site visits in Northern Virginia (October 2025)
In October 2025, ICE announced site visits to employers of F-1 students in Northern Virginia. HSI reported evidence of employer fraud. It found worksites that did not function or were staged, and supervisors who did not know the rules for employing foreign students. One employer said it ran dozens of OPT positions from a home in the Virginia suburbs. ICE said it would step up investigations to reduce fraud and security risks in the OPT program.

## Paying for fake proof of employment is visa fraud
In July 2019, ICE announced the indictment of a businesswoman who set up two companies, Findream LLC and Sinocontech LLC. The companies did not provide any services and did not employ the people who answered their ads. For a fee, they gave customers written proof of employment so the customers could stay in the US on student and work visas. At least 2,685 customers listed these companies as their employer, and the companies received at least $2 million. Each visa fraud count carries up to 10 years in prison.
Source: https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/chinese-businesswoman-indicted-student-visa-work-visa-fraud-charges

## How fake-employer schemes recruited students
According to ICE, the 2019 scheme advertised on a China-based job website and in a WeChat group aimed at job hunters in North America. The customers were F-1 students seeking to extend their employment authorization, and people seeking H-1B work visas. In this scheme the job seeker paid the "employer" for paperwork, which is the reverse of real employment. Prosecutors charged the scheme as conspiracy to commit visa fraud and visa fraud.
Source: https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/chinese-businesswoman-indicted-student-visa-work-visa-fraud-charges

## Students arrested for claiming nonexistent employers
In October 2020, ICE announced Operation OPTical Illusion. Fifteen nonimmigrant students were arrested for fraudulently using the OPT program. They claimed to work for companies that do not exist. Arrests took place in several states and the Washington, DC area. ICE said it would continue checking whether students who got jobs through OPT were complying with their immigration status. Listing a fake or paid-for employer puts the student, not just the company, at legal risk.
