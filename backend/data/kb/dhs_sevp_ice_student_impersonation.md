## DHS will never ask for money this way
In a February 2026 advisory, DHS warned that scammers pose as DHS officials to target foreign students. DHS includes USCIS, ICE, Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) and the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP). The advisory says DHS will never ask you to transfer money to an individual person. It also says DHS will never ask you to pay fees by phone or by email. A caller or emailer who asks for either is not acting for DHS.

## Common tactics in DHS imposter scams
According to DHS, scammers may ask for your Alien Registration Number or Form I-94. They may say there is a problem with your record in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS). They may demand payment by wire transfer, gift card, prepaid debit card, credit card or cryptocurrency, or ask for bank or card details. Some promise to "fix" your immigration status for a fee. These calls may show a real government number on caller ID, threaten arrest or deportation, and push you to act right away.

## How to recognize real DHS officials
Real HSI representatives, including SEVP field representatives, carry official credentials that show their photo and title. You may ask to see these credentials. If a credential seems suspicious, contact your Designated School Official (DSO) or the SEVP Response Center at 703-603-3400. Real DHS emails come from addresses ending in ".gov". They include a full signature with the sender's name, phone number and .gov email address.

## What students should do
DHS advises students who suspect a scam not to share personal or financial information and not to send money. End the call or conversation. Contact your DSO at your school's international student office. Report the contact to the ICE Tip Line at 1-866-347-2423. DSOs are asked to contact their SEVP field representative or the SEVP Response Center and to report suspicious contacts to the ICE Tip Line.

## The DSO's role and SEVIS records
A Designated School Official is a school employee chosen by the head of an SEVP-certified school. Federal law requires DSOs to update and maintain F and M student records in SEVIS. DSOs also advise students on work authorization, travel, address changes, program extensions and other status matters. A student who is told there is a problem with their SEVIS record should check with their DSO before taking any other step. A paid caller cannot "fix" a SEVIS record.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/schools/get-started/designated-school-official

## SEVP never asks for gift cards or bitcoin
A December 2022 Study in the States alert described callers posing as SEVP Response Center staff. They told students to buy gift cards, which an "agent" would supposedly collect. SEVP stated that its officials will never ask for credit card, debit card or gift card numbers, wire transfers or bank routing numbers, or ask anyone to make bitcoin deposits. The scam calls used a 703 area code. SEVP said its real calls to students come from the Washington, DC 202 area code.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2022/12/students-beware-of-phone-scammers

## Scammers spoof real SEVP phone numbers
In December 2020, SEVP warned that scammers were spoofing the real SEVP Response Center numbers, 703-603-3400 and 800-892-4829. The callers asked students about their Form I-94 and tried to get financial information. A familiar SEVP number on caller ID does not prove a call is real. SEVP advised students not to share personal or financial details with unknown callers and to contact their DSO if unsure. It also pointed victims to the HSI tip form at ice.gov.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2020/12/students-watch-out-for-scammers-using-sevp-phone-numbers

## What real SEVP Portal emails look like
Students on post-completion OPT or STEM OPT use the SEVP Portal to report their address, phone number and employer. Portal emails come from do-not-reply.SEVP@ice.dhs.gov. They cover account creation, temporary passwords, password changes, locked accounts and email changes. Each one says not to reply because the mailbox is not monitored. For help, the emails point to the SEVP Response Center at 703-603-3400 or SEVP@ice.dhs.gov.
Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/sevis-and-the-sevp-portal/portal-email-notices

## HSI agents do not arrest people by phone
In October 2024, HSI in Michigan and Ohio warned about people posing as HSI special agents. The callers claimed a package containing money or drugs had been intercepted in an investigation involving the victim. HSI said its agents do not call to demand account information or payment, do not threaten jail and offer payment as an alternative, and do not try to arrest people by phone. They also do not ask for Social Security, credit card or bank account numbers over the phone.
Source: https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/hsi-michigan-and-ohio-warn-scammers-impersonating-special-agents

## Passport "problem" calls and spoofed HSI lines
In November 2021, HSI San Antonio warned that imposters were spoofing its main office number, 210-979-4500. The callers claimed the victim's passport was involved in a crime and threatened to send police to arrest them. HSI said its special agents and local police do not call people to warn them of an upcoming arrest. They do not ask for bank or credit card information, and they do not demand money to drop an investigation or cancel a warrant.
Source: https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/hsi-san-antonio-warns-public-about-spoofing-phone-calls

## How to reach the ICE Tip Line
The ICE Tip Line is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week at 866-DHS-2-ICE (866-347-2423) from the US and Canada. From other countries, call (802) 872-6199. Tips can also be sent through the online form at ice.gov/webform/ice-tip-form. Staff speak several languages and have access to interpreters. HSI also runs its own tip line at 877-4-HSI-TIP (1-877-447-4847) for reporting people who pose as special agents.
Source: https://www.ice.gov/tipline
